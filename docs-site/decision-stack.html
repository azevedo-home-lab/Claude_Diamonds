Decision Stack — Eriksson

The vertical why: five connected layers from vision to everyday principles, read down for how and up for why — and one test that finds the first broken link, which is exactly where the missing work is.

2 primary sources 8 sections Sources: The Decision Stack · Mind the Product talk
01

The whole method on one page

Five layers, each answering one question — connected, or they are just five posters.

The stack ladder: every pair of layers is connected twice — down as how, up as why. The how/why test walks any item up and stops at the first link that needs a paragraph.

Two sentences to remember. Nothing is justified by pointing sideways — only by pointing up. And: the layers change at different rates — a vision that changes monthly and objectives that never change are both broken.
02

Why: the stance

Eriksson's diagnosis: not an execution problem — a clarity problem.

Most organizations have some form of vision, strategy, and well-intentioned objectives. The problem is that none of it connects. When the connection is absent, choices that should have been made once instead get made thousands of times, by hundreds of people, inconsistently. The stack is not a planning ritual; it is a machine for making each decision once, at the right layer, so every layer below inherits it instead of re-deciding it.

The stack's economic argument: a decision made once at the right layer is inherited everywhere below; a decision made at no layer is re-made inconsistently forever.

Three beliefs underneath. First, alignment is what buys autonomy: upper layers are meaningful constraints, and inside them people can move fast without asking. Second, clarity = transparency + understanding — a published strategy nobody can apply to their next decision is transparency without clarity. Third, each layer has its own tempo: vision nearly static, strategy revised on evidence, objectives evolving continuously, opportunities churning weekly. A stack whose layers all change at the same rate — usually zero — is a poster, not a stack.

03

Building blocks

Five layers, one question each — and for each, the look-alike to reject.

LayerThe question it answersChangesLook-alikes to reject
Vision / MissionWhere are we going, and why does it matter?Rarely, if everA revenue target; a feature list with adjectives
StrategyHow will we get there? Where to play, how to winOn evidence, not on moodA strategy that excludes nothing — that's a wish
ObjectivesWhat is important right now?Per cycleOutputs ("ship X") dressed as objectives
Opportunities & SolutionsWhat actions are we taking?ContinuouslySolutions with no named opportunity above them
PrinciplesHow do we make everyday choices without escalating?SlowlyValues posters — a principle without an "even over" is a platitude

The vision is the fixed point the rest of the stack triangulates against — allowed to be unreachable, not allowed to be vague about why it matters. A strategy is a set of explicit trade-offs; its test is what it rules out — if no reasonable person would choose the opposite, it isn't a choice. Objectives are outcomes — what changes for whom — never outputs: "ship the verifier" is a plan item; "merge on a green verdict without re-reading the diff" is an objective. Opportunities come before solutions because solutions serve specific opportunities — this layer is where the Opportunity Solution Tree lives. Principles are best written as trade-offs — "X even over Y" — because a principle that never costs anything never decides anything.

04

The machine

No ceremony to install — three recurring moves, worked on the running example: one product, outcome "merge on a green verdict without re-reading the diff."

Move 1 — Write the stack down, one layer at a time

Each layer in the product's own words. Short is a feature: a layer entry that doesn't fit a table cell is hiding an unmade decision.

LayerThe worked entry
VisionA solo builder ships like a team: work is delegated to an AI workflow and the result is trusted without redoing it by hand
StrategyWin on verifiable trust (proof artifacts, fresh evidence) even over speed; play in single-builder repos even over team platforms
ObjectivesNorth Star: share of merges landing on a green verdict with the diff unread. Inputs: fake-green incidents (target 0) · median verification time · share of stalls with a named cause
Opportunities"Done is not proven done" — the target from the tree; siblings "no signal of progress" and "found work never filed" wait
PrinciplesFresh evidence even over reported success · challenge before agreeing · append-only memory even over tidy files

Move 2 — The how/why test, before any promotion

Walk the candidate up the stack, one sentence per link. Two walks below: a bet that passes, and a proposal that fails at its very first link — the failure IS the finding.

Link"Fresh-context verifier" (bet)"Template marketplace" (proposal)
Initiative → BetOne of three compared candidates under the "done is not proven done" bet.BREAKS — no bet names it; the justification points sideways at a competitor, not up. This link is the missing work.
Bet → ObjectivesA verdict that must show fresh evidence makes a green trustworthy enough to merge on unread.never reached
Objectives → StrategyMerges-on-green measures whether verifiable trust — the chosen way to win — is actually delivered.never reached
Strategy → VisionWinning on verifiable trust is how delegation stops costing the trust it saves in labor.never reached

Each link one sentence. The verifier walks to the top; the marketplace fails at the first link — and the honest response is to say so plainly, not to write the paragraph that papers over it.

The test's output is a location, not a verdict. A failed walk doesn't kill the idea — it names the layer where work is missing. Sometimes the marketplace idea is right and the strategy is what needs the update — decided at the strategy layer, once, on evidence.

Move 3 — Route every incoming proposal to its layer

Locate the proposal on the stack before discussing its merits. A feature request is solution-level: walk it up until it hits a named bet or fails to. "We should focus more on X" is strategy-level: resolve it there, once — never re-litigate it per initiative. A recurring escalation ("do we take shortcuts under deadline?") is a missing principle: write the "even over" and stop deciding it weekly.

The failure modes

The connective failures from Eriksson's framing plus the structural ones the operating file guards — scan them by name against the stack, the way the ten traps are scanned against a rank.

#Failure modeWhat it looks likeRemedy
1The everything-strategyStrategy that excludes nothingName what you will NOT do; a strategy is its exclusions
2Output objective"Ship X by Q3" as an objectiveReformulate: who does what measurably differently
3Strategy vacuumObjectives set against no (or stale) strategyThe vacuum is the blocking gap; do not prioritize into it
4Sideways justification"Competitor has it" / "the tech is elegant"Only pointing up justifies; re-run the how/why walk
5The floating objectiveAn objective no strategy sentence explainsConnect it or kill it — an orphan objective is invented work
6Layer skippingVision straight to featuresThe skipped layers are where the thousand daily decisions leak
7Per-initiative relitigationStrategy debated again on every itemResolve at the strategy layer once; record it
8Principles as postersValues with no "even over"Rewrite as trade-offs, or delete them
9The frozen stackEvery layer changes at the same rate: zeroEach layer has its own tempo; a stack is used, not laminated
10Decided a thousand timesThe same small call escalated weeklyThe missing principle — write it once
Solo-portfolio regulars: 2, 3, 4, 10. One builder plus an AI produces output-objectives and sideways justifications faster than a team does — the AI will happily elaborate either. Scan those four at minimum, every session.
06

The OST & Cutler handoffs

The stack is the frame the other two methods run inside — one vertical spine, two horizontal machines.

QuestionMethodWhere on the stack it runs
Does this point up?Decision StackThe vertical spine — all layers
What's worth betting on?OST (Torres)Opportunities & Solutions, expanded into a tree
What first?Cutler rankOrdering within the Opportunities layer

The OST's root outcome IS the Objectives-layer entry — the tree expands one layer of the stack into evidence and compared solutions. When a solution survives its assumption tests and leaves the tree as a bet, the Cutler rank orders it against the other bets — and the rank's "value" lens (which moves the outcome more?) is an upward-pointing question: the how/why test worn as a comparator. Neither handoff works if the stack above is broken — a tree rooted in an output, or a rank argued against a strategy vacuum, inherits the defect.

07

In Claude Diamonds

How the front diamond applies the stack, per product. The binding rules live in decision-stack.claude.md.

Eriksson layerConcrete artifact
Vision / MissionThe vision line of the product's stack — published into /plan/stack.md at the gate
StrategyThe strategy section of the same stack — explicit trade-offs, revised only on evidence
ObjectivesNorth Star + input metrics — a standing outcome metric instead of quarterly OKRs
Opportunities & SolutionsThe force-ranked bets (portfolio grain) and their initiatives (solution level)
PrinciplesNo per-product layer — the project instructions and the method itself are the shared principles layer

The operating rules, in one paragraph: the how/why test runs before any promotion — item to bet, bet to strategy, strategy to vision, each link one sentence, the first paragraph-length link named plainly as the missing work. Every incoming proposal is located on the stack before its merits are discussed. Output-objectives are reformulated as outcomes before prioritizing. An empty or stale Strategy layer is the blocking gap — nothing is prioritized into a vacuum. Strategy disagreements are resolved at the strategy layer, never re-litigated per initiative. And nothing is justified by pointing sideways — at a competitor, a technology, an idea's elegance — only by pointing up.

08

Self-test

If you can answer these from memory, you can run the stack — solo or in a room.

  1. Name the five layers in order, and the question each answers.
  2. State the how/why test in two sentences. What exactly does the first unstateable link tell you — and why is it a location, not a verdict?
  3. Why must a strategy exclude something? What is a strategy that excludes nothing?
  4. "Ship the verifier by March" — which failure mode fires, and what is the reformulation?
  5. Why do the layers change at different rates, and what does it mean when they all change at the same rate?
  6. A proposal is justified by "our main competitor just launched this." Which rule fires, and what would the legitimate version of the argument look like?
  7. In Claude Diamonds, which artifact plays the Objectives layer, and how does it differ from quarterly OKRs?
  8. Where does the OST plug into the stack, and what defect does a tree inherit if the layer above it is broken?
  9. The same shortcut-vs-quality argument recurs every week. Which layer is missing its entry, and in what form should the entry be written?
