## The stance

Cutler is framework-skeptical, not framework-free. Scoring systems (RICE,
weighted matrices) tend toward false rigor: weights and scores used to
avoid the difficult conversation, with the loop on outcomes never closed.
What replaces them: force-ranked **bets** (1‑N, not buckets of "high"),
argued through **value vs urgency**, **opportunity cost** ("you can't
prioritize something unless you deprioritize something else"), and **cost
of delay** (what does a month of waiting cost — and is it linear, or does
it fall off a cliff?). The aim is marginally better, good-enough decisions
made quickly and revisited — not a perfect ordering.

Prioritization is also only a small piece: some uncomfortable share of
prioritized work fails to produce its intended outcome, so each bet needs
an owner, an expected outcome, and a checkpoint where the loop is closed.

## The ten traps (TBM 399) — condensed with remedies

1. **Burning down the house** — all capacity reactive → protect a fixed
   slice (10–20%) for preventative work.
2. **Too much time on my hands** — over-polishing medium-value work →
   define the smallest version that teaches something real in 2–4 weeks.
3. **Everybody wants to rule the world** — "top priority" with no focus →
   name one thing explicitly allowed to drop, out loud.
4. **Just enough is never enough** — MVP shipped, value never captured →
   pre-authorize one follow-on investment.
5. **Running on empty** — death march, no off-track signal → set a date,
   learning milestone, or decision checkpoint in advance.
6. **Dreamer** — innovation with no constraints → add one enabling
   constraint (deadline to ship real, forced integration).
7. **Slow ride** — invisible systemic friction never prioritized →
   quantify the drag; reframe as cost of delay, not cleanup.
8. **Someday never comes** — big options deferred for lack of confidence
   → run one cheap experiment aimed only at reducing uncertainty.
9. **The Logical Song** — frameworks favor high-confidence, untestable
   work → add the column "how cheaply can we raise confidence?"; favor
   fast learning.
10. **Takin' care of business** — invented work fills capacity → keep a
    small-batch pull queue of accepted, needle-moving work.

## How Claude applies this in the front diamond

- The zone's output is a force-ranked 1‑N list of bets per product, then
  folded into Now / Next / Later for communication. Buckets are the
  presentation; the rank is the decision. Ties are not allowed; a tie
  means the deciding conversation hasn't happened — name what it is.
- Each ranked bet states, in one line each: expected outcome (traced to
  the stack), cost of delay (and its shape: linear or cliff), what is
  explicitly deprioritized to fund it, and the checkpoint where the loop
  closes (date or learning milestone).
- Impact-vs-effort is the entry sort, not the verdict: after sorting,
  apply the confidence question (trap 9) — cheap-to-learn beats
  comfortable-to-defend — and check the ranking against the traps.
- Trap check at every prioritization session: scan the current list for
  traps 1, 3, 7, and 8 by name (the four a solo portfolio hits most) and
  say which apply. A "top priority" that displaces nothing is flagged.
- Claude does not present scores as decisions. Numbers may inform the
  conversation; the decision-log entry records the argument (value,
  urgency, opportunity cost), not a score.
- Re-ranking is cheap and expected; relitigating a DECIDED rank without
  new evidence is not. New evidence reopens via a new log entry.
